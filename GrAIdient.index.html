The GrAIdient

Develop your initial stance on AI in education. Identify an assignment, break it into the steps a student would take, then decide how you’d feel if a student used AI for each step.

Step 1: Identify a student assignment

Think of a significant assignment you or a colleague would give in one of your classes. Assignments with several steps work best.

Need an idea? Browse sample assignments

Select one to use it. You can edit it to fit your class.

Describe an assignment to continue.

Step 2: Break the assignment into steps

List each step a student would need to take to complete this assignment, one step per sticky note. Aim for about 5–10 steps.

Example 1: Create an informational poster about animals that live in the Arctic
  1. Choose an Arctic animal to research.
  2. Find information in books and on websites.
  3. Take notes in your own words.
  4. Decide which facts are most important to include.
  5. Sketch a layout for the poster.
  6. Write headings and captions.
  7. Draw or find images.
  8. Check facts and spelling.
  9. Present the poster to the class.
Example 2: Write an analytical essay
  1. Read and annotate the text.
  2. Brainstorm possible topics or questions.
  3. Write a thesis statement.
  4. Gather evidence and quotations that support the thesis.
  5. Create an outline.
  6. Write a first draft.
  7. Get feedback from a peer.
  8. Revise for argument and structure.
  9. Edit grammar, style, and citations.

Step 3: Map and reflect

Step 3a: Place the steps on the GrAIdient

What if students used AI to help with these steps? For each step, reflect on your own comfort level, then drag its sticky note onto the continuum, from Totally fine to Crosses a line.

Keyboard: focus a note, press 1–5 to place it, ← → to fine-tune, Delete to return it.

Step 3b: Explain your reasoning

Choose a few steps, especially those in the gray area or ones you went back and forth on, and explain why you placed them where you did.

Questions to guide your notes

  • Why did you place this step here?
  • What learning goal are you trying to protect?
  • Would AI support the student’s thinking here, or replace it?
  • What would make this use acceptable? What would make it cross a line?
